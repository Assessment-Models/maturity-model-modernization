# The Central Proposition: Assessing Modernization vs Architecting Modernization

The most consequential part of the framework is that it separates **the assessment of modernization** from **the architecture of modernization**.

| | Assessment of modernization | Architecture of modernization |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Determine modernization need | Determine how the need is realized |
| Character | Common, consistent, comparable, evidence-based | Domain-specific, contextual, designed |
| Produces | Modernization opportunities, priorities, direction | Architectural implications, decisions, capabilities, interventions, realization paths |
| Scope | Enterprise, portfolio, domain, system, or component level, with one method | The specific architecture disciplines affected by the opportunity |

## Why this matters

An organization can assess modernization at an enterprise level, a portfolio level, a domain level, a system level, or an individual architectural component level, without inventing a new assessment methodology for each architecture discipline.

The common assessment determines what needs to change, why, how significantly, and toward what desired state. Architecture-specific contextualization determines what that means for each affected architecture: the changes, capabilities, decisions, interventions, and realization paths.

## The unifying principle

> One modernization assessment foundation. Multiple architectural interpretations. Coordinated realization.

The organization assesses modernization once through a common framework, contextualizes the findings across affected architecture disciplines, and coordinates the resulting interventions through a coherent modernization strategy.

## Formalization path

The framework formalizes as distinct but interconnected artifacts:

1. Assessment framework foundation (purpose, scope, principles, terminology, normative boundaries).
2. Assessment taxonomy (the eight dimensions, subdimensions, definitions, domain applicability).
3. Criterion catalog (assessment criteria as modeled propositions).
4. Assessment mechanics (scales, methods, scoring, confidence, evidence sufficiency, weighting, aggregation).
5. Modernization interpretation (findings, context, risk, value, and target expectations generating opportunities and priorities).
6. Architecture domain contextualization (fan-out to domain-specific implications, concerns, interventions).
7. Modernization realization model (objectives to decisions, capabilities, initiatives, transition plans, outcomes, value).
8. Assessment instrument and validation.

The recommended sequence is to formalize the normative taxonomy and criterion catalog first, before scoring or maturity progression, to validate that the assessment covers architecture modernization holistically rather than prematurely locking into an assessment instrument.
