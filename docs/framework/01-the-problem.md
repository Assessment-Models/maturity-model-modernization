# The Problem: Fragmented Modernization Assessment

## How modernization is usually assessed

Architecture modernization is often approached through the lens of a specific architecture discipline:

- An enterprise architect assesses enterprise architecture.
- A business architect assesses business architecture.
- A technology team assesses infrastructure.
- Software teams assess applications and software architecture.

These perspectives are individually valid but collectively fragmented. They may:

- Identify overlapping modernization needs.
- Use different assessment criteria.
- Recommend conflicting priorities.
- Produce initiatives that are not traceable to a common modernization objective.

## The reframing

Modernization is not inherently an enterprise architecture, technology architecture, software architecture, or systems architecture initiative. It is an **organizational change opportunity** that manifests across one or more architecture domains, depending on:

- What needs to change.
- Why it needs to change.
- What outcomes the change is expected to achieve.

## The central proposition

> Architecture modernization should be assessed through a common, architecture-agnostic framework that establishes the nature, urgency, scope, value, and direction of modernization, and subsequently contextualizes the assessment into the specific architectural domains, capabilities, decisions, and interventions required to realize it.

This establishes two distinct but connected assessment concerns:

| Concern | Question answered |
|---------|-------------------|
| Common modernization assessment | What needs to modernize, why, how significantly, and toward what desired state? |
| Architecture-specific contextualization | What does that modernization opportunity mean for each affected architecture, and what changes, capabilities, decisions, and interventions are required? |

The separation makes it possible to compare modernization opportunities consistently without erasing the technical and contextual differences between architectural disciplines.
