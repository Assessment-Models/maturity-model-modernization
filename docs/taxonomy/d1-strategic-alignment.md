# D1 · Strategic Alignment & Relevance

**Assessment objective**: determine whether the architecture remains relevant to the organization's strategy, value proposition, operating model, and future ambition.

## Subdimensions and assessment criteria

| Subdimension | Assessment criteria |
|--------------|---------------------|
| D1.1 Strategic Alignment | Alignment with organizational strategy, objectives, strategic priorities, and investment direction |
| D1.2 Business Relevance | Relevance to business model, value proposition, products, services, and stakeholder needs |
| D1.3 Value Contribution | Contribution to value creation, value delivery, value capture, and business outcomes |
| D1.4 Future Alignment | Ability to support anticipated strategic direction and future business models |
| D1.5 Priority & Criticality | Importance of the architecture to strategic execution and business continuity |

## Modernization signals

- The architecture constrains strategic execution.
- The organization is pursuing strategic objectives that the current architecture cannot support.
- Architectural investment is disconnected from business priorities.
- New business models or value propositions require architectural change.
- The architecture no longer provides sufficient differentiation or strategic leverage.

## Interpretation notes

D1 establishes the **why** of modernization. A weak D1 with strong condition elsewhere does not indicate complacency; it often indicates that the architecture has drifted from the strategy it was built to serve. D1.4 (Future Alignment) is assessed as future demand, not current condition, and should be captured distinctly from current strategic alignment.
