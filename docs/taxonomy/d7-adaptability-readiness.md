# D7 · Adaptability & Modernization Readiness

**Assessment objective**: assess how easily the architecture can evolve and how prepared the organization is to undertake modernization.

## Subdimensions and assessment criteria

| Subdimension | Assessment criteria |
|--------------|---------------------|
| D7.1 Changeability | Ease, speed, cost, and risk of modifying architecture elements |
| D7.2 Extensibility | Ability to accommodate new capabilities, requirements, technologies, and use cases |
| D7.3 Migration & Transition | Ability to transition from current to target state with manageable disruption |
| D7.4 Engineering & Delivery Readiness | Development, engineering, automation, testing, deployment, and delivery capacity |
| D7.5 Organizational Readiness | Skills, ownership, governance, sponsorship, resources, and operating readiness |
| D7.6 Modernization Constraints | Dependencies, contractual obligations, legacy constraints, funding, policy, and transition barriers |

## Modernization signals

- Changes take disproportionately long or are excessively expensive.
- Modernization cannot be executed incrementally.
- The organization lacks the skills, governance, or delivery mechanisms to realize architectural change.
- Dependencies create significant transition risk.
- The architecture cannot adapt to emerging needs.

## Interpretation notes

A crucial distinction: an architecture may be in poor condition but **easy to modernize**, or in relatively good condition but **extremely difficult to change**. These must not be conflated.

D7 measures the **feasibility side** of modernization, and readiness is deliberately kept separate from priority. A highly critical but hard-to-execute opportunity is not deprioritized; it may require preparatory initiatives, risk mitigation, capability development, or staged modernization. Readiness findings therefore translate into the **shape of the path**, while D1-D6 findings establish the need to walk it.
