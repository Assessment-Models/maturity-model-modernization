# Modernization Assessment, Not a Maturity Model

AMAF is a distinct assessment model, not an architecture maturity model. The distinction is meaningful:

| | Architecture Maturity Assessment | Architecture Modernization Assessment |
|---|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Evaluates | Maturity of an architecture or architectural practice | Modernization need, opportunity, direction, and realization potential |
| Primarily concerned with | Maturity and progression | Change, fitness, value, risk, and future demand |
| Identifies | Maturity gaps | Modernization opportunities and implications |
| Produces | Maturity improvement recommendations | Modernization objectives, interventions, and architectural change |
| Progression | Maturity-level driven | Context, need, value, and target-state driven |

## Maturity as an input, not an organizing principle

Maturity may still be a useful **input** to the modernization assessment, particularly where architectural governance, engineering, organizational, or delivery capability constrains modernization (this is captured in D7, Adaptability & Modernization Readiness). However, maturity should not become the organizing principle of the modernization assessment.

## Why this matters

A maturity model asks "how mature is our architecture practice?" AMAF asks "what needs to change, why, how significantly, and toward what state?" The first question measures position on a ladder that may not lead anywhere in particular. The second establishes the modernization opportunity, its significance, and its realization path. Confusing the two produces improvement programs that climb levels without traceability to a modernization objective.
